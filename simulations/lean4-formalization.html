The Principle of Stationary Action

The principle of stationary action states that the physical path \(q_{true}\) is a critical point of \(S[q]\): \[ \delta S = 0 \iff D S(q_{true})[\eta] = 0 \quad \text{for all variations } \eta \]

Deriving Euler-Lagrange: The Fundamental Lemma of Calculus of Variations

A crucial step in the physics derivation is the Fundamental Lemma of Calculus of Variations: If \(\int_{t_1}^{t_2} f(t) \eta(t) dt = 0\) for all smooth functions \(\eta(t)\) vanishing at the boundaries, then \(f(t) = 0\) everywhere.

Formalizing this lemma in Lean 4 requires: 1. Constructing bump functions (which are available in mathlib’s smooth manifolds library). 2. Using measure theory to show that if the integral of \(f \cdot \eta\) is zero for all bump functions, the almost-everywhere equivalence class of \(f\) is zero. 3. Using the continuity of \(f\) to upgrade “almost everywhere zero” to “everywhere zero”.
