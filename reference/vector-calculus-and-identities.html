Vector Algebra

Let \(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}, \mathbf{C}\) be vectors in 3D space.

Dot and Cross Products

\[ \mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = A_x B_x + A_y B_y + A_z B_z = |\mathbf{A}| |\mathbf{B}| \cos\theta \] \[ \mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B} = \det \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{\hat{i}} & \mathbf{\hat{j}} & \mathbf{\hat{k}} \\ A_x & A_y & A_z \\ B_x & B_y & B_z \end{vmatrix} \] \[ |\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B}| = |\mathbf{A}| |\mathbf{B}| \sin\theta \]

Triple Products

Scalar Triple Product (Volume of parallelepiped): \[ \mathbf{A} \cdot (\mathbf{B} \times \mathbf{C}) = \mathbf{B} \cdot (\mathbf{C} \times \mathbf{A}) = \mathbf{C} \cdot (\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B}) = \det \begin{vmatrix} A_x & A_y & A_z \\ B_x & B_y & B_z \\ C_x & C_y & C_z \end{vmatrix} \]

Vector Triple Product (BAC-CAB Rule): \[ \mathbf{A} \times (\mathbf{B} \times \mathbf{C}) = \mathbf{B}(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{C}) - \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B}) \]

Differential Vector Calculus (Nabla Operator \(\nabla\))

The del operator in Cartesian coordinates: \[ \nabla = \mathbf{\hat{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x} + \mathbf{\hat{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y} + \mathbf{\hat{k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z} \]

Operations

  1. Gradient (Scalar \(f \to\) Vector): \[ \nabla f = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\mathbf{\hat{i}} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\mathbf{\hat{j}} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\mathbf{\hat{k}} \]
  2. Divergence (Vector \(\mathbf{A} \to\) Scalar): \[ \nabla \cdot \mathbf{A} = \frac{\partial A_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial A_y}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial A_z}{\partial z} \]
  3. Curl (Vector \(\mathbf{A} \to\) Vector): \[ \nabla \times \mathbf{A} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{\hat{i}} & \mathbf{\hat{j}} & \mathbf{\hat{k}} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ A_x & A_y & A_z \end{vmatrix} \]
  4. Laplacian (Scalar \(f \to\) Scalar): \[ \nabla^2 f = \nabla \cdot (\nabla f) = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial z^2} \]

Vector Identities

Product Rules

\[ \nabla(fg) = f\nabla g + g\nabla f \] \[ \nabla \cdot (f\mathbf{A}) = f(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A}) + \mathbf{A} \cdot (\nabla f) \] \[ \nabla \times (f\mathbf{A}) = f(\nabla \times \mathbf{A}) + (\nabla f) \times \mathbf{A} \] \[ \nabla \cdot (\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B}) = \mathbf{B} \cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{A}) - \mathbf{A} \cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{B}) \] \[ \nabla(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B}) = \mathbf{A} \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{B}) + \mathbf{B} \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{A}) + (\mathbf{A} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{B} + (\mathbf{B} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{A} \]

Second Derivatives

Curl of a Gradient is zero: \[ \nabla \times (\nabla f) = 0 \] (If \(\nabla \times \mathbf{F} = 0\), then \(\mathbf{F}\) is conservative and \(\mathbf{F} = -\nabla U\).)

Divergence of a Curl is zero: \[ \nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{A}) = 0 \] (If \(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0\), then \(\mathbf{B}\) can be written as the curl of a vector potential \(\mathbf{A}\).)

Curl of a Curl: \[ \nabla \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{A}) = \nabla(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A}) - \nabla^2\mathbf{A} \]

Integral Theorems

Divergence Theorem (Gauss’s Theorem)

Relates a volume integral to a surface integral over the closed boundary \(S\): \[ \iiint_V (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A}) \, dV = \iint_S \mathbf{A} \cdot d\mathbf{a} \]

Stokes’ Theorem

Relates a surface integral to a line integral around the closed bounding curve \(C\): \[ \iint_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{A}) \cdot d\mathbf{a} = \oint_C \mathbf{A} \cdot d\mathbf{l} \]

Gradient Theorem (Fundamental Theorem of Calculus for Line Integrals)

\[ \int_a^b (\nabla f) \cdot d\mathbf{l} = f(b) - f(a) \]

# Vector Algebra

Let $\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}, \mathbf{C}$ be vectors in 3D space.

## Dot and Cross Products
$$ \mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = A_x B_x + A_y B_y + A_z B_z = |\mathbf{A}| |\mathbf{B}| \cos\theta $$
$$ \mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B} = \det \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{\hat{i}} & \mathbf{\hat{j}} & \mathbf{\hat{k}} \\ A_x & A_y & A_z \\ B_x & B_y & B_z \end{vmatrix} $$
$$ |\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B}| = |\mathbf{A}| |\mathbf{B}| \sin\theta $$

## Triple Products
**Scalar Triple Product** (Volume of parallelepiped):
$$ \mathbf{A} \cdot (\mathbf{B} \times \mathbf{C}) = \mathbf{B} \cdot (\mathbf{C} \times \mathbf{A}) = \mathbf{C} \cdot (\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B}) = \det \begin{vmatrix} A_x & A_y & A_z \\ B_x & B_y & B_z \\ C_x & C_y & C_z \end{vmatrix} $$

**Vector Triple Product (BAC-CAB Rule)**:
$$ \mathbf{A} \times (\mathbf{B} \times \mathbf{C}) = \mathbf{B}(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{C}) - \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B}) $$

# Differential Vector Calculus (Nabla Operator $\nabla$)

The del operator in Cartesian coordinates:
$$ \nabla = \mathbf{\hat{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x} + \mathbf{\hat{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y} + \mathbf{\hat{k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z} $$

## Operations
1. **Gradient** (Scalar $f \to$ Vector):
   $$ \nabla f = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\mathbf{\hat{i}} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\mathbf{\hat{j}} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\mathbf{\hat{k}} $$
2. **Divergence** (Vector $\mathbf{A} \to$ Scalar):
   $$ \nabla \cdot \mathbf{A} = \frac{\partial A_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial A_y}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial A_z}{\partial z} $$
3. **Curl** (Vector $\mathbf{A} \to$ Vector):
   $$ \nabla \times \mathbf{A} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{\hat{i}} & \mathbf{\hat{j}} & \mathbf{\hat{k}} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ A_x & A_y & A_z \end{vmatrix} $$
4. **Laplacian** (Scalar $f \to$ Scalar):
   $$ \nabla^2 f = \nabla \cdot (\nabla f) = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial z^2} $$

# Vector Identities

## Product Rules
$$ \nabla(fg) = f\nabla g + g\nabla f $$
$$ \nabla \cdot (f\mathbf{A}) = f(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A}) + \mathbf{A} \cdot (\nabla f) $$
$$ \nabla \times (f\mathbf{A}) = f(\nabla \times \mathbf{A}) + (\nabla f) \times \mathbf{A} $$
$$ \nabla \cdot (\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B}) = \mathbf{B} \cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{A}) - \mathbf{A} \cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{B}) $$
$$ \nabla(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B}) = \mathbf{A} \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{B}) + \mathbf{B} \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{A}) + (\mathbf{A} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{B} + (\mathbf{B} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{A} $$

## Second Derivatives
**Curl of a Gradient is zero:**
$$ \nabla \times (\nabla f) = 0 $$
*(If $\nabla \times \mathbf{F} = 0$, then $\mathbf{F}$ is conservative and $\mathbf{F} = -\nabla U$.)*

**Divergence of a Curl is zero:**
$$ \nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{A}) = 0 $$
*(If $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$, then $\mathbf{B}$ can be written as the curl of a vector potential $\mathbf{A}$.)*

**Curl of a Curl:**
$$ \nabla \times (\nabla \times \mathbf{A}) = \nabla(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A}) - \nabla^2\mathbf{A} $$

# Integral Theorems

## Divergence Theorem (Gauss's Theorem)
Relates a volume integral to a surface integral over the closed boundary $S$:
$$ \iiint_V (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A}) \, dV = \iint_S \mathbf{A} \cdot d\mathbf{a} $$

## Stokes' Theorem
Relates a surface integral to a line integral around the closed bounding curve $C$:
$$ \iint_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{A}) \cdot d\mathbf{a} = \oint_C \mathbf{A} \cdot d\mathbf{l} $$

## Gradient Theorem (Fundamental Theorem of Calculus for Line Integrals)
$$ \int_a^b (\nabla f) \cdot d\mathbf{l} = f(b) - f(a) $$
