Calculus of Variations: Problem Solutions

This article contains problem statements and solution outlines for Chapter 7 of John R. Taylor’s Classical Mechanics. For a theoretical overview, see the Chapter 7 Lecture Notes.

Section 7.1: Lagrange’s Equations for Unconstrained Motion

Problem 7.1 *

Statement: Write down the Lagrangian for a projectile (subject to no air resistance) in terms of its Cartesian coordinates \((x, y, z)\), with \(z\) measured vertically upward. Find the three Lagrange equations and show that they are exactly what you would expect.
(See Section 7.1 Notes)

Solution: \(\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}m(\dot{x}^2 + \dot{y}^2 + \dot{z}^2) - mgz\). The equations are: - \(x\): \(\frac{d}{dt}(m\dot{x}) = 0 \implies \ddot{x} = 0\) - \(y\): \(\frac{d}{dt}(m\dot{y}) = 0 \implies \ddot{y} = 0\) - \(z\): \(\frac{d}{dt}(m\dot{z}) = -mg \implies \ddot{z} = -g\) These are the standard equations of motion for a projectile.

Problem 7.2 *

Statement: Write down the Lagrangian for a one-dimensional particle moving along the \(x\) axis and subject to a force \(F = -kx\) (with \(k\) positive). Find the Lagrange equation of motion and solve it.

Solution: \(\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}^2 - \frac{1}{2}kx^2\). Equation: \(-kx = m\ddot{x} \implies \ddot{x} + \omega^2 x = 0\). Solution is simple harmonic motion \(x(t) = A\cos(\omega t - \delta)\).

Problem 7.3 *

Statement: Consider a mass \(m\) moving in two dimensions with potential energy \(U(x, y) = \frac{1}{2}kr^2\), where \(r^2 = x^2 + y^2\). Write down the Lagrangian and find the two Lagrange equations of motion. Describe their solutions.

Solution: \(\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}m(\dot{x}^2 + \dot{y}^2) - \frac{1}{2}k(x^2 + y^2)\). Equations: \(m\ddot{x} = -kx\) and \(m\ddot{y} = -ky\). Both \(x\) and \(y\) undergo independent simple harmonic motion with the same frequency.

Problem 7.4 *

Statement: Mass \(m\) moving in a frictionless plane that slopes at angle \(\alpha\). Write down the Lagrangian in terms of \(x\) (horizontally across slope) and \(y\) (down slope).

Solution: \(\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}m(\dot{x}^2 + \dot{y}^2) + mgy\sin\alpha\). Equations: \(m\ddot{x} = 0\) and \(m\ddot{y} = mg\sin\alpha\).

Problem 7.6 & 7.7 *

Statement: Show that the \(3N\) Newtonian equations are exactly equivalent to the \(3N\) Lagrange equations in Cartesian coordinates, establishing Hamilton’s principle.

Section 7.3: Constrained Systems in General

Problem 7.9 *

Statement: Bead threaded on a rigid circular hoop of radius \(R\) in the \(xy\) plane. Use the angle \(\phi\). Write equations giving \((x, y)\) in terms of \(\phi\).
(See Section 7.3 Notes)

Solution: \(x = R\cos\phi, \quad y = R\sin\phi\). Generalized coordinate is just \(\phi\).

Problem 7.10 *

Statement: Particle on the surface of a circular cone, axis on \(z\), half-angle \(\alpha\).

Solution: Using cylindrical \(( \rho, \phi, z )\): \(z = \rho \cot \alpha\). The coordinates are \((\rho, \phi)\). \(x = \rho\cos\phi\), \(y = \rho\sin\phi\), \(z = \rho\cot\alpha\).

Section 7.4: Proof of Lagrange’s Equations with Constraints

Problem 7.12 * (Friction)

Statement: Modify Lagrange’s equations to include a nonconservative force \(F_{\text{fric}}\).
(See Section 7.4 Notes)

Solution: \(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial x} + F_{\text{fric}} = \frac{d}{dt} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{x}}\).

Problem 7.13 **

Statement: Prove Lagrange’s equations for two particles subject to various constraints, using the property that constraint forces do zero net work.

Section 7.5: Examples of Lagrange’s Equations

Problem 7.14 * (Yoyo)

Statement: Massless string wrapped around a uniform cylinder (mass \(m\), radius \(R\)). Find acceleration.

Solution: \(\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}mR^2)\omega^2 - (-mgx)\). Since \(x = R\phi\), \(\omega = \dot{x}/R\). \(\mathcal{L} = \frac{3}{4}m\dot{x}^2 + mgx\). Equation: \(mg = \frac{3}{2}m\ddot{x} \implies \ddot{x} = \frac{2}{3}g\).

Problem 7.21 *

Statement: Rod forced to rotate in horizontal plane at constant \(\omega\). Bead on rod.

Solution: \(\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}m(\dot{r}^2 + r^2\omega^2)\). Equation: \(m\ddot{r} = mr\omega^2\). Shows centrifugal force accelerating the bead exponentially.

Section 7.8: More About Conservation Laws

Problem 7.46 ** (Noether’s Theorem for Rotations)

Statement: Prove that rotational invariance of \(\mathcal{L}\) implies conservation of total angular momentum.
(See Section 7.8 Notes)

Solution: Rotating by \(\epsilon\) around the \(z\)-axis changes coordinates. If \(\delta \mathcal{L} = 0\), the sum of \(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \phi_\alpha} = 0 \implies \sum \dot{p}_{\phi_\alpha} = 0 \implies L_z\) is conserved.

Section 7.9: Lagrange’s Equations for Magnetic Forces

Problem 7.49 **

Statement: Charge \(q\) in uniform magnetic field \(B \hat{z}\). Prove \(B = \nabla \times A\) and find Lagrange equations.
(See Section 7.9 Notes)

Solution: \(A = \frac{1}{2} B \times r\). The Lagrangian involves the vector potential, leading directly to the Lorentz force equation.

Section 7.10: Lagrange Multipliers and Constraint Forces

Problem 7.50 * & 7.51 *

Statement: Atwood machine and pendulum solved using Lagrange multipliers \(\lambda\) to find string tensions.
(See Section 7.10 Notes)

Solution: For the pendulum, constraint \(f(x, y) = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = l\). The multiplier \(\lambda\) represents the constraint force (tension), yielding equations that simultaneously solve the motion and the dynamic tension in the rod.

## Section 7.1: Lagrange's Equations for Unconstrained Motion

### Problem 7.1 *
**Statement:** Write down the Lagrangian for a projectile (subject to no air resistance) in terms of its Cartesian coordinates $(x, y, z)$, with $z$ measured vertically upward. Find the three Lagrange equations and show that they are exactly what you would expect.  
*(See [Section 7.1 Notes](../notes/taylor-chapter-7-lagrange-equations.qmd#71-lagranges-equations-for-unconstrained-motion))*

**Solution:**
$\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}m(\dot{x}^2 + \dot{y}^2 + \dot{z}^2) - mgz$.
The equations are:
- $x$: $\frac{d}{dt}(m\dot{x}) = 0 \implies \ddot{x} = 0$
- $y$: $\frac{d}{dt}(m\dot{y}) = 0 \implies \ddot{y} = 0$
- $z$: $\frac{d}{dt}(m\dot{z}) = -mg \implies \ddot{z} = -g$
These are the standard equations of motion for a projectile.

### Problem 7.2 *

Equation: $-kx = m\ddot{x} \implies \ddot{x} + \omega^2 x = 0$. Solution is simple harmonic motion $x(t) = A\cos(\omega t - \delta)$.

## Section 7.8: More About Conservation Laws

### Problem 7.46 ** (Noether's Theorem for Rotations)
**Statement:** Prove that rotational invariance of $\mathcal{L}$ implies conservation of total angular momentum.  
*(See [Section 7.8 Notes](../notes/taylor-chapter-7-lagrange-equations.qmd#78-conservation-laws-and-noethers-theorem))*

**Solution:**
Rotating by $\epsilon$ around the $z$-axis changes coordinates. If $\delta \mathcal{L} = 0$, the sum of $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \phi_\alpha} = 0 \implies \sum \dot{p}_{\phi_\alpha} = 0 \implies L_z$ is conserved.

## Section 7.9: Lagrange's Equations for Magnetic Forces

### Problem 7.49 **
**Statement:** Charge $q$ in uniform magnetic field $B \hat{z}$. Prove $B = \nabla \times A$ and find Lagrange equations.  
*(See [Section 7.9 Notes](../notes/taylor-chapter-7-lagrange-equations.qmd#79-lagranges-equations-for-magnetic-forces))*

**Solution:**
$A = \frac{1}{2} B \times r$. The Lagrangian involves the vector potential, leading directly to the Lorentz force equation.

**Solution:**
For the pendulum, constraint $f(x, y) = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = l$. The multiplier $\lambda$ represents the constraint force (tension), yielding equations that simultaneously solve the motion and the dynamic tension in the rod.
