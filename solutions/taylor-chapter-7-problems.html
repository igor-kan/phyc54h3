SECTION 7.1 Lagrange’s Equations for Unconstrained Motion

(See Section 7.1 Notes)

Problem 7.1 *

Statement: Write down the Lagrangian for a projectile (subject to no air resistance) in terms of its Cartesian coordinates (x, y, z), with z measured vertically upward. Find the three Lagrange equations and show that they are exactly what you would expect for the equations of motion.

(See Section 7.1 Notes)

Solution: \(\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}m(\dot{x}^2 + \dot{y}^2 + \dot{z}^2) - mgz\). The equations are: - \(x\): \(\frac{d}{dt}(m\dot{x}) = 0 \implies \ddot{x} = 0\) - \(y\): \(\frac{d}{dt}(m\dot{y}) = 0 \implies \ddot{y} = 0\) - \(z\): \(\frac{d}{dt}(m\dot{z}) = -mg \implies \ddot{z} = -g\) These are the standard equations of motion for a projectile.

Problem 7.2 *

Statement: Write down the Lagrangian for a one-dimensional particle moving along the x axis and subject to a force F = — kx (with k positive). Find the Lagrange equation of motion and solve it.

Solution: \(\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}^2 - \frac{1}{2}kx^2\). Equation: \(-kx = m\ddot{x} \implies \ddot{x} + \omega^2 x = 0\). Solution is simple harmonic motion \(x(t) = A\cos(\omega t - \delta)\).

Problem 7.3 *

Statement: Consider a mass in moving in two dimensions with potential energy U (x, y) = Zkr 2 , where r2 = x 2 + y 2 . Write down the Lagrangian, using coordinates x and y, and find the two Lagrange equations of motion. Describe their solutions. [This is the potential energy of an ion in an “ion trap,” which can be used to study the properties of individual atomic ions.]

Solution: \(\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}m(\dot{x}^2 + \dot{y}^2) - \frac{1}{2}k(x^2 + y^2)\). Equations: \(m\ddot{x} = -kx\) and \(m\ddot{y} = -ky\). Both \(x\) and \(y\) undergo independent simple harmonic motion with the same frequency.

### Problem 7.1 *

**Statement:** Write down the Lagrangian for a projectile (subject to no air resistance) in terms of its Cartesian coordinates (x, y, z), with z measured vertically upward. Find the three Lagrange equations and show that they are exactly what you would expect for the equations of motion.

*(See [Section 7.1 Notes](../notes/taylor-chapter-7-lagrange-equations.qmd#71-lagranges-equations-for-unconstrained-motion))*

**Solution:**
$\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}m(\dot{x}^2 + \dot{y}^2 + \dot{z}^2) - mgz$.
The equations are:
- $x$: $\frac{d}{dt}(m\dot{x}) = 0 \implies \ddot{x} = 0$
- $y$: $\frac{d}{dt}(m\dot{y}) = 0 \implies \ddot{y} = 0$
- $z$: $\frac{d}{dt}(m\dot{z}) = -mg \implies \ddot{z} = -g$
These are the standard equations of motion for a projectile.
