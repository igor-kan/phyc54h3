1. Generalized Coordinates and Degrees of Freedom

In Newtonian mechanics, a system of \(N\) particles moving in 3D requires \(3N\) Cartesian coordinates \(\mathbf{r}_i\). However, physical systems are often subject to \(k\) geometric holonomic constraints: \[f_\alpha(\mathbf{r}_1, \dots, \mathbf{r}_N, t) = 0 \quad (\alpha = 1, \dots, k)\]

The number of unconstrained degrees of freedom (DOF) is: \[n = 3N - k\]

We introduce \(n\) independent generalized coordinates \(\mathbf{q} = (q_1, q_2, \dots, q_n)^T\) that automatically satisfy the constraints: \[\mathbf{r}_i = \mathbf{r}_i(q_1, \dots, q_n, t)\]

The particle velocities are expressed via the multivariable chain rule: \[\mathbf{v}_i = \dot{\mathbf{r}}_i = \sum_{j=1}^n \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}_i}{\partial q_j} \dot{q}_j + \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}_i}{\partial t}\]

Notice the crucial cancellation of dots identity: \[\frac{\partial \dot{\mathbf{r}}_i}{\partial \dot{q}_j} = \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}_i}{\partial q_j}\]

2. Hamilton’s Principle and the Euler-Lagrange Equations

For a conservative system with kinetic energy \(T(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}}, t)\) and potential energy \(V(\mathbf{q}, t)\), the Lagrangian is: \[\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}}, t) = T - V\]

Hamilton’s principle states that the actual motion \(\mathbf{q}(t)\) between fixed times \(t_1\) and \(t_2\) makes the action stationary: \[\delta S = \delta \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}}, t) \, dt = 0\]

This yields the \(n\) coupled second-order differential equations: \[\boxed{\frac{d}{dt}\left( \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{q}_j} \right) - \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q_j} = 0 \quad (j = 1, \dots, n)}\]

Generalized Momentum and Cyclic Coordinates

  • Generalized Momentum: \(p_j \equiv \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{q}_j}\)
  • Generalized Force: \(Q_j \equiv \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q_j}\)
  • Noether’s Theorem for Cyclic Coordinates: If the Lagrangian does not explicitly depend on a coordinate \(q_k\) (\(\partial \mathcal{L} / \partial q_k = 0\)), \(q_k\) is said to be cyclic or ignorable. Then: \[\frac{d}{dt}\left( \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{q}_k} \right) = 0 \implies \boxed{p_k = \text{constant}}\] Every spatial symmetry of \(\mathcal{L}\) directly produces a conservation law.

## 1. Generalized Coordinates and Degrees of Freedom

In Newtonian mechanics, a system of $N$ particles moving in 3D requires $3N$ Cartesian coordinates $\mathbf{r}_i$. However, physical systems are often subject to $k$ geometric **holonomic constraints**:
$$f_\alpha(\mathbf{r}_1, \dots, \mathbf{r}_N, t) = 0 \quad (\alpha = 1, \dots, k)$$

The number of unconstrained **degrees of freedom (DOF)** is:
$$n = 3N - k$$

We introduce $n$ independent **generalized coordinates** $\mathbf{q} = (q_1, q_2, \dots, q_n)^T$ that automatically satisfy the constraints:
$$\mathbf{r}_i = \mathbf{r}_i(q_1, \dots, q_n, t)$$

The particle velocities are expressed via the multivariable chain rule:
$$\mathbf{v}_i = \dot{\mathbf{r}}_i = \sum_{j=1}^n \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}_i}{\partial q_j} \dot{q}_j + \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}_i}{\partial t}$$

Notice the crucial **cancellation of dots** identity:
$$\frac{\partial \dot{\mathbf{r}}_i}{\partial \dot{q}_j} = \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}_i}{\partial q_j}$$

## 2. Hamilton's Principle and the Euler-Lagrange Equations

For a conservative system with kinetic energy $T(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}}, t)$ and potential energy $V(\mathbf{q}, t)$, the **Lagrangian** is:
$$\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}}, t) = T - V$$

Hamilton's principle states that the actual motion $\mathbf{q}(t)$ between fixed times $t_1$ and $t_2$ makes the action stationary:
$$\delta S = \delta \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}}, t) \, dt = 0$$

This yields the $n$ coupled second-order differential equations:
$$\boxed{\frac{d}{dt}\left( \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{q}_j} \right) - \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q_j} = 0 \quad (j = 1, \dots, n)}$$

### Generalized Momentum and Cyclic Coordinates

*   **Generalized Momentum:** $p_j \equiv \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{q}_j}$
*   **Generalized Force:** $Q_j \equiv \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q_j}$
*   **Noether's Theorem for Cyclic Coordinates:** If the Lagrangian does not explicitly depend on a coordinate $q_k$ ($\partial \mathcal{L} / \partial q_k = 0$), $q_k$ is said to be **cyclic** or **ignorable**. Then:
    $$\frac{d}{dt}\left( \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{q}_k} \right) = 0 \implies \boxed{p_k = \text{constant}}$$
    Every spatial symmetry of $\mathcal{L}$ directly produces a conservation law.
