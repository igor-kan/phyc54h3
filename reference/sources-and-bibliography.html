Textbooks & Course Literature

This repository references several classic and foundational textbooks for mathematical methods, classical mechanics, and general physics.

Core Mechanics

Classical Mechanics (2005)

Author: John R. Taylor Publisher: University Science Books Description: The primary textbook for PHYC54H3. Known for its conversational tone and extreme clarity, it builds from elementary Newtonian mechanics to advanced topics like the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formalisms, central-force motion, and chaos theory. Link: University Science Books - Taylor

Introduction to Classical Mechanics: With Problems and Solutions (2008)

Author: David Morin Publisher: Cambridge University Press Description: Highly regarded for its vast collection of very challenging problems, making it a staple for physics Olympiad preparation. The textbook emphasizes clever problem-solving techniques and covers the Lagrangian method extensively. Link: Cambridge University Press - Morin

The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Volume I: Mainly Mechanics, Radiation, and Heat

Authors: Richard P. Feynman, Robert B. Leighton, and Matthew Sands Publisher: Basic Books (Online edition by Caltech) Description: The legendary lecture series by Nobel laureate Richard Feynman. It approaches classical mechanics with unmatched physical intuition, emphasizing deep conceptual understanding over rote mathematics. Link: Feynman Lectures Vol. I

Mechanics (Course of Theoretical Physics, Volume 1)

Authors: L. D. Landau and E. M. Lifshitz Publisher: Elsevier (Butterworth-Heinemann) Description: A hallmark of Russian theoretical physics, this dense and mathematically elegant text begins directly with the principle of least action and develops the entirety of mechanics deductively. Ideal for advanced students. Link: Elsevier - Landau & Lifshitz

Mathematical Methods of Classical Mechanics (Graduate Texts in Mathematics, Vol. 60)

Author: V. I. Arnold Publisher: Springer Description: A classic graduate-level textbook that provides a modern, geometric approach to classical mechanics. It builds mechanics from the ground up using the geometry of manifolds, symplectic geometry, and Lie groups. Link: Springer - Arnold

Mathematical Methods

Mathematical Methods for Physics and Engineering

Authors: K. F. Riley, M. P. Hobson, and S. J. Bence Publisher: Cambridge University Press Description: An immensely practical guide covering all undergraduate mathematical requirements, from basic calculus and vectors to complex variables, tensors, and group theory. Link: Cambridge University Press - Riley

Mathematical Methods for Physicists

Authors: George B. Arfken and Hans J. Weber Publisher: Academic Press (Elsevier) Description: Known as the “bible” of mathematical physics, this comprehensive text goes extremely deep into vector analysis, differential equations, and special functions. Link: Elsevier - Arfken

Mathematical Methods in the Physical Sciences

Author: Mary L. Boas Publisher: John Wiley & Sons Description: An approachable but rigorous text that bridges the gap between basic calculus and the advanced mathematics required in upper-level physics courses. Link: Wiley - Boas

General Physics

Physics, Volume 1 (5th Edition)

Authors: Robert Resnick, David Halliday, Kenneth S. Krane Publisher: John Wiley & Sons Description: The definitive introductory calculus-based physics text. This classical tome is incredibly thorough on mechanics, thermodynamics, and waves. Link: Wiley - Krane

Online Open Courses

MIT OCW 8.01SC Classical Mechanics (Fall 2016)

Description: A spectacular open courseware resource from MIT, emphasizing problem-solving and rigorous foundational mechanics. Contains lecture notes, problem sets, and video lectures. Link: MIT 8.01SC Classical Mechanics

Fundamentals of Physics I (PHYS 200) - Open Yale Courses

Instructor: Prof. Ramamurti Shankar Description: A complete, filmed introductory physics course covering Newtonian mechanics, special relativity, and thermodynamics. Shankar’s lectures are famous for their engaging and insightful delivery. Link: Open Yale Courses - PHYS 200

MIT OCW 8.012 Physics I: Classical Mechanics (Fall 2008)

Description: An advanced version of the standard introductory mechanics course at MIT, aimed at students with strong math backgrounds. It explores Newtonian mechanics with greater rigor. Link: MIT 8.012 Classical Mechanics

MIT OCW 8.223 Classical Mechanics II (January IAP 2017)

Description: A fast-paced, intensive course focusing heavily on the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formulations of classical mechanics, central forces, and rigid body dynamics. Link: MIT 8.223 Classical Mechanics II

MIT OCW 8.09 Classical Mechanics III (Fall 2014)

Description: An advanced undergraduate/graduate level course covering chaotic dynamics, canonical transformations, Hamilton-Jacobi theory, and continuum mechanics. Link: MIT 8.09 Classical Mechanics III

Computational References

Python Programming and Numerical Methods: A Guide for Engineers and Scientists

Authors: Qingkai Kong, Timmy Siauw, Alexandre Bayen Description: An open-access textbook (Jupyter Notebooks) from UC Berkeley detailing numerical Python (NumPy, SciPy) methods for ODEs, root finding, and basic data analysis. Link: Berkeley Python Numerical Methods

# Computational References

**Authors:** Qingkai Kong, Timmy Siauw, Alexandre Bayen
**Description:** An open-access textbook (Jupyter Notebooks) from UC Berkeley detailing numerical Python (NumPy, SciPy) methods for ODEs, root finding, and basic data analysis.
